# TabGNS on TabM

**Question.** TabGNS (Orucu et al., NOMS 2026) searches the widths of a plain MLP by putting a learnable
Gumbel gate on every hidden neuron and training the gates on validation batches, alternating with the
weights. Does the method carry over to TabM (Gorishniy et al., ICLR 2025), a parameter-efficient
ensemble of k MLPs that share their weight matrices? In particular: does it find TabMs that are
clearly smaller than TabM's default architecture without losing accuracy, as it does for MLPs?

**Short answer.** Yes, it transfers, but it compresses TabM far less than it compresses an MLP, and on
two of the three datasets what it really contributes is *the size*, not the weights or the shape. Full
tables: [`results/tabm_gns/summary.md`](../../results/tabm_gns/summary.md) (generated by `report.py`).

## Results

Three seeds each, mean ± sample std; Δ is the paired difference to the Large model of the same
backbone. "Size" counts every trainable parameter, including TabM's 32 sets of adapters and heads.

| dataset (metric) | Large TabM | TabGNS-TabM, shared mask | size | TabGNS-TabM, per-member masks | size |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| California (RMSE ↓) | 0.1392 ± 0.0023 | 0.1369 ± 0.0031 (Δ −0.0023, better 3/3) | 19.8% | 0.1365 ± 0.0025 (Δ −0.0027, better 3/3) | 19.5% |
| Adult (accuracy ↑) | 85.98 ± 0.16 | 85.80 ± 0.02 (Δ −0.18) | 3.5% | 85.88 ± 0.15 (Δ −0.09) | 3.9% |
| Otto (accuracy ↑) | 78.96 ± 0.33 | 78.83 ± 0.36 (Δ −0.13) | 32.4% | 78.94 ± 0.30 (Δ −0.02) | 41.2% |

For reference, on the same splits and features, TabGNS on the replication's 5 × 512 MLP:

| dataset | Large MLP | TabGNS-MLP | size |
|---|---|---|---|
| California | 0.1546 ± 0.0009 | 0.1518 ± 0.0021 | 1.3% |
| Adult | 85.57 ± 0.04 | 85.29 ± 0.18 | 0.9% |
| Otto | 74.73 ± 0.73 | 75.69 ± 0.59 | 1.9% |

**1. TabGNS finds smaller TabMs that are as good as the default.** On all three datasets the found TabM
is within about one standard deviation of Large TabM at 3.5–41% of its parameters: slightly better
on California (every seed), 0.1–0.2 pp lower accuracy on Adult (log-loss 0.307 vs 0.304), equal on
Otto (log-loss slightly better: 0.545 / 0.547 vs 0.549). TabM is the better backbone throughout:
TabGNS-TabM beats TabGNS-MLP on every dataset (by about 3 pp accuracy on Otto) at 2.6–17× its size.

**2. TabM is compressed much less than an MLP.** TabGNS keeps 0.9–1.9% of a Large MLP but 3.5–41% of
a Large TabM. The ensemble size is the main reason, not the dataset alone: with a shared mask, the
found TabM grows with k on California (4.7% of Large at k = 1, 11% at k = 4, 20% at k = 32) and on
Adult (1.4%, 1.9%, 3.5%), while staying as good as Large TabM of the same k
(`results/tabm_gns/ablation_k*`; three seeds per k). A plausible reason, not tested: a neuron's row of
the shared W serves k differently scaled members, so closing it costs more validation loss in a
larger ensemble. The search's stopping rule also sets the size: TabGNS stops on the validation loss,
and on California and Otto the TabM networks are still losing neurons when it stops
([`width_during_search.png`](../../results/tabm_gns/figures/width_during_search.png)), while the MLP
searches shrink much faster. A longer search patience might find smaller TabMs (not tested).

**3. Shared vs. per-member gate noise makes no consistent difference** in accuracy. Per-member masks
keep larger networks on Otto (41% vs 32%).

**4. What the supernet contributes: on California and Adult, only the size.** The controls retrain the
found widths in three ways with the TabM training protocol and a shared data order. *Rewind*: the found
widths initialised with the supernet's initial weights of the kept neurons (the same seed, so exactly
the weights the search started from). *Scratch*: the found widths from a fresh initialisation.
*Uniform*: equal widths in every layer, chosen to match the found model's parameter count.

| dataset | variant | warm start (the method) | rewind | scratch | uniform width | Large TabM |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| California | shared mask | 0.1369 ± 0.0031 | 0.1365 ± 0.0020 | 0.1368 ± 0.0021 | 0.1375 ± 0.0015 | 0.1392 ± 0.0023 |
| California | per-member | 0.1365 ± 0.0025 | – | 0.1361 ± 0.0020 | 0.1366 ± 0.0016 | 0.1392 ± 0.0023 |
| Adult | shared mask | 85.80 ± 0.02 | 85.96 ± 0.17 | 85.98 ± 0.12 | 85.96 ± 0.09 | 85.98 ± 0.16 |
| Adult | per-member | 85.88 ± 0.15 | 86.07 ± 0.19 | 86.06 ± 0.03 | 86.04 ± 0.11 | 85.98 ± 0.16 |
| Otto | shared mask | 78.83 ± 0.36 | *deferred* | 77.62 ± 0.53 | 77.69 ± 0.30 | 78.96 ± 0.33 |
| Otto | per-member | 78.94 ± 0.30 | – | 77.99 ± 0.38 | 78.09 ± 0.40 | 78.96 ± 0.33 |

On California and Adult all four are within noise of each other; on Adult the three retrained
versions are even 0.15–0.19 pp *better* than the warm-started one. So there the supernet's trained
weights add nothing, its initial weights (rewind) add nothing over a fresh initialisation, and the
uneven widths add nothing over equal widths with the same parameter count. A plain TabM of the found
size does as well, and better than Large TabM on California. TabGNS is useful there as a one-shot
**size selector** for TabM: it found that California needs about a fifth of Large TabM and Adult about
a thirtieth, without a tuning loop.

**5. On Otto, the supernet's weights matter.** Retraining the found widths from scratch loses about
1 pp of accuracy (−1.21 / −0.95 pp; 0/3 seeds as good as the warm start), and equal widths of the same
size lose as much (−1.14 / −0.85 pp, 0/3), so the warm start, not the shape, carries Otto. Whether
this is the supernet's *initial* weights (a lottery-ticket effect) or its *training* is what the
rewind control answers; it needs the Otto searches replayed and is deferred (below). A caveat that the rewind control will not remove: the
warm-started model has seen far more training (140–250 search epochs plus 120–190 fine-tuning epochs)
than a single retraining, and 5 of the 12 Otto retrainings stopped at the 300-epoch cap, so part of the
gap may be training budget rather than anything the search found.

**Baselines.** The comparisons are the default (Large) TabM, the controls, and TabGNS on an MLP. The
replication's other NAS baselines (TabNAS, AgE) were not run on TabM, and the TabM paper's numbers
are not comparable (it tunes hyperparameters per dataset and uses numerical embeddings; the splits
differ). A tuned-width TabM (e.g. a width search with Optuna) is the natural stronger baseline.

## What TabM is, and where the gates go

TabM (`arch_type='tabm'` in the reference package `tabm` 0.0.3) runs k = 32 MLPs in parallel on the
same input. Every linear layer is a BatchEnsemble layer: one weight matrix W shared by all members,
plus per-member input scaling r_i, output scaling s_i and bias b_i,

    y_i = s_i ⊙ (W (r_i ⊙ x_i)) + b_i,

followed by ReLU and dropout. Each member has its own linear head; the prediction is the mean of the
members' probabilities (or outputs), and training minimises the mean of the members' losses. The
first layer's r is initialised with random ±1 signs (one per input feature), all other r and s with
ones, and the k biases start equal. The defaults are 3 blocks of 512 units, k = 32, dropout 0.1.

`experiments/tabm_gns/model.py` re-implements this so that every layer can be gated and pruned. A unit
test copies the weights of a reference `tabm.TabM.make(...)` into it and checks that the outputs and
parameter counts match.

**Gate placement.** One TabGNS gate per hidden neuron, applied after ReLU and dropout, exactly where
`tabgns.gates.GatedBlock` applies it. **A gate is shared by all k members.** A neuron of a TabM layer is
a row of the shared W together with one entry of every member's s_i and b_i and one column of the next
layer's W and r_i. Removing it for some members but not others would turn the shared W into k
different matrices and lose what makes TabM cheap, so the architecture TabGNS searches is one list of
widths for the whole ensemble.

**Gate noise (the one TabM-specific choice).** TabGNS draws one hard Gumbel sample per gate per forward
pass. In TabM there are two natural readings, and both are run:

- `tabm_gns` (**shared mask**): one mask per pass for the whole ensemble. This is TabGNS unchanged.
- `tabm_gns_member` (**per-member masks**): every member draws its own mask from the same gate
  logits. Each step then sees k = 32 sub-networks of the same distribution, which averages the gate
  gradient over more samples. It also means the members are trained on different sub-networks.

**Extraction.** As in `TabGNS.extract_net`: keep neurons with sigmoid(g) ≥ 0.5, copy their rows of W
and columns of r, s, b and the heads; with `scale: true`, multiply the kept neurons' s and b by
sigmoid(g), which equals TabGNS's scaling of W's rows and b because sigmoid(g) > 0 commutes with ReLU.
Unit tests check that the extracted TabM reproduces the supernet with the corresponding fixed mask to
1e-6, with and without scaling.

## Protocol

| | value | source |
|---|---|---|
| datasets | California (OpenML 44025, regression, 20,640 × 8), Adult (1590, binary, 48,842 × 14, 8 categorical), Otto (45548, 9 classes, 61,878 × 93) | all three are in the TabM benchmark; none is used by the replication |
| split | 60/20/20, split seed = run seed | as `experiments.datasets.split` |
| preprocessing | fitted on the training split only: quantile-normal numeric features, one-hot categoricals (missing = own level), standardised regression target | train-only fitting as in `experiments/mean_gate` |
| TabM | 3 × 512, k = 32, dropout 0.1, no numerical embeddings | `tabm.TabM.make` defaults |
| TabM training | AdamW lr 2e-3, weight decay 3e-4, batch 256, ≤ 300 epochs, early stopping (patience 20) on the ensemble's validation loss, best epoch restored | lr/wd from the `tabm` README; epochs/patience/batch from the replication |
| search space | the Large TabM itself (3 × 512) | |
| TabGNS search | weight step on a training batch, no-grad validation-loss evaluation on the next validation batch (eval mode), gate step on that batch (still eval mode); Adam lr 0.05 for gates, gate logit init −3, temperature 1, logits [g, 1]; early stopping (patience 20) on the epoch's mean validation-batch loss; the **last** epoch's net is used | all as `tabgns.TabGNS.fit` and `conf/method/tabgns.yaml`, including its quirks (P(open) = sigmoid(g − 1) while sampling, last-epoch checkpoint) |
| search weights | same optimiser and dropout as TabM training | |
| after the search | extract, warm-start fine-tune with the TabM training protocol; also train the found widths from a fresh initialisation ("scratch") | fine-tuning as `TabGNSTrainer` |
| controls | rewind, scratch and uniform-width retrainings of each found TabM (see Results, finding 4), same TabM training protocol; rewind, scratch and uniform share one data order | |
| pairing | within a seed, the supernet starts from exactly the Large TabM's initial weights (unit test); data order, gate noise and the scratch init use separate derived random streams | |
| MLP reference | `mlp_large` (5 × 512) and `mlp_gns` call `experiments.methods.run_large_mlp` / `run_tabgns` unchanged, with the replication's protocol (Adam/AdamW lr 1e-3, no dropout), on the same splits and preprocessed features | |
| seeds | 0, 1, 2 | |
| metrics | test accuracy (and log-loss) for classification; test RMSE for California, in the dataset's units log(1 + price / $100k) | |
| parameters | "params" = all trainable parameters; for TabM that is shared W + the 32 members' r, s, b + the 32 heads. "hidden" = hidden-to-hidden weights (the paper's count) | |

The California target on OpenML 44025 is log(1 + median house value / $100k) (the Grinsztajn et al.
benchmark's transformation), so its RMSE is not comparable to RMSEs in dollars.

## Reproduce

```bash
python -m unittest -v experiments.tabm_gns.test_tabm_gns   # the parity test needs `pip install tabm`
python -m experiments.tabm_gns.run -m hydra/launcher=joblib hydra.launcher.n_jobs=7 \
    dataset=otto,adult,california method=tabm_gns,tabm_gns_member,tabm_large,mlp_gns,mlp_large seed=0,1,2
python -m experiments.tabm_gns.report
```

Runs that saved their masks (`seedN.masks.npz`) train the rewind and uniform controls right after
their own result. For runs without masks, `method=tabm_controls` (shared mask) replays the search to
recover the masks and checks the replay against the stored histories; `method=tabm_controls_member`
trains only the uniform control. `method.rewind=false` skips the replay. The queues actually used are
`queue_today.txt` and `queue_deferred.txt` (one Hydra override line per cell, run with `xargs -P`;
lock files let several queues share cells, and finished cells are skipped):

```bash
xargs -P 6 -L 1 sh -c '.venv/bin/python -m experiments.tabm_gns.run $0 "$@"' < experiments/tabm_gns/queue_today.txt
```

The ensemble-size ablation (California and Adult, shared mask, results in
`results/tabm_gns/ablation_k1` and `ablation_k4`):

```bash
python -m experiments.tabm_gns.run -m hydra/launcher=joblib hydra.launcher.n_jobs=2 dataset=california,adult \
    method=tabm_gns,tabm_large tabm.k=1,4 seed=0,1,2 'experiment=tabm_gns/ablation_k${tabm.k}'
```

Each job runs one (dataset, method, seed) cell on one CPU thread and writes
`results/tabm_gns/<dataset>/<method>/seed<seed>.json` with the config, metrics on validation and test,
widths, parameter counts, per-epoch architecture and loss histories, timings and provenance. Cells
that already have a result are skipped.
